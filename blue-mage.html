Blue Mage

Blue Mages learn magic from monsters: by surviving their attacks, or by studying what's left of them.

v0.1 PlaytestArcane Tradition: Blue MagicInspired by Final Fantasy

Inspired by the saber-wielding Blue Mage of Final Fantasy Tactics Advance 2, they fight on the front line because that's where the lessons are. Blue Magic uses existing spells from any class's list; the Blue Magic table maps monster abilities to those spells.

Level 3

Saber Training

You gain proficiency with Martial melee weapons that lack the Two-Handed and Heavy properties, and you can use such a weapon as a Spellcasting Focus for your Wizard spells.

Level 3

Blue Magic

You can learn spells from any class's spell list, but only from creatures. Spells learned this way are your Blue Magic spells. They count as Wizard spells for you, and you record them in your spellbook at no gold cost. You learn Blue Magic in two ways:

  • Learn by Being Hit. When a creature hits you with an ability on the Blue Magic table, or forces you to make a saving throw against one, you can record the matching spell when the encounter ends, provided you don't have the Unconscious condition.
  • Learn by Study. During a Short or Long Rest, you can spend 1 hour studying the remains of a creature and make an Intelligence (Arcana) check (DC 10 + the creature's CR). On a success, you record one spell that creature can teach. A creature's remains can teach only one spell, and only one attempt can be made on them; to learn more of a creature's Blue Magic, you must find another one. The remains must be largely intact.

You can record Blue Magic of any level, but you can't cast a spell until you have spell slots of its level.

Level 3

Azure Repertoire

You can prepare a number of additional Blue Magic spells equal to your Proficiency Bonus. These don't count against the number of spells you can prepare.

Level 6

Extra Attack

You can attack twice instead of once whenever you take the Attack action on your turn. You can replace one of those attacks with a casting of one of your Wizard cantrips that has a casting time of an action.

Level 6

Libra

As a Bonus Action, choose a creature you can see within 60 feet of yourself. You learn its damage Vulnerabilities, Resistances, and Immunities, its Condition Immunities, and which Blue Magic spells it can teach you. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum of once), and you regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

Level 10

I've Felt This Before

When a creature uses an ability whose matching spell is recorded in your spellbook, you have Advantage on saving throws against that ability and Resistance to its damage.

Level 14

Azure Retort

When you're hit by a Blue Magic ability, or fail a saving throw against one, you can take a Reaction to cast the matching spell at that creature without expending a spell slot, provided the spell is recorded in your spellbook and you can cast spells of its level. Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest unless you expend a level 5 spell slot to restore your use of it.

Blue Magic Table

The DM has final say on which abilities can teach which spells. The tables below are suggestions, not a complete list; DMs are encouraged to map other creatures and abilities the same way.

  • Spellcasters. A creature with the Spellcasting action can teach any spell it lists. Those spells don't need table entries.
  • Innate abilities. Everything else uses the tables below, organized by creature type. The same ability can appear at several levels, depending on the creature's size and age.
  • Study only. Abilities that don't hit you or force you to save, such as a will-o'-wisp turning invisible, can be learned only by study.
  • CR cap. A creature can teach Blue Magic of a level no higher than half its CR, rounded up. A CR 0 or 1/2 creature teaches only cantrips.
  • Beyond the cap. You can learn a spell up to the creature's signature level even when it exceeds the CR cap, but it takes an Intelligence (Arcana) check at DC 10 + the creature's CR, plus 5 for each level the spell exceeds the cap. When studying, this replaces the normal study DC. When learning by being hit, you make the check when the encounter ends; on a failure, you can still try again later by studying the creature. Once you have spell slots of the spell's level, the +5 penalty no longer applies, so a medusa can still teach Flesh to Stone to a Blue Mage who can already cast level 6 spells.
  • Kindred Summons. A creature can teach the Summon spell matching its creature type (Summon Beast, Summon Undead, Summon Fiend, and so on), subject to the normal CR cap.
  • Warrior Techniques. Martial humanoids teach fighting techniques, mapped to smite, strike, and other combat spells from the Paladin and Ranger lists. A DM can map any warrior NPC's signature move the same way.

Playtest notes
  • Learning pace: is the party meeting enough teachable creatures to make a new spell feel like a regular event?
  • Study DC: check that DC 10 + CR feels reachable at low and mid levels.
  • Off-list access: watch for any non-Wizard spell that becomes too strong in a Wizard's hands.
  • Frontline survivability: with no defensive feature, see whether the Blue Mage survives melee at low levels.
  • Azure Retort: confirm a free reflected spell at level 14 isn't too swingy with high-level Blue Magic.
  • Stat block check: verify ability names and CRs in the tables against the 2024 Monster Manual.
  • Divine Smite: a Blue Mage with Divine Smite and Extra Attack could hit hard; watch how often devas appear and whether it dominates.
